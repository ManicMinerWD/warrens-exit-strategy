Option 1 Bali — Income Strategy

PT PMA registration → Mandiri bank account → IDX domestic shares (Mandiri / Indonesian listed equity) · rental income 10% · stay onshore in IDR · capital gains 0.1% final tax

Last updated:

Strategy overview the flow: villa → PT PMA → IDR bank → IDX domestic shares (in-country, in IDR)

Rental income from the Bali villa lands in an Indonesian PT PMA bank account (Mandiri). A 10% final income tax is withheld on gross rental receipts. Rather than move money offshore (which would trigger an FX conversion, an outbound dividend withholding, and a foreign capital gains tax), the after-tax IDR stays onshore in Indonesia and is deployed into the Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX / Bursa Efek Indonesia — BEI) — specifically into Indonesian-listed domestic shares, for example Bank Mandiri (Mandiri stock) or another Indonesian-listed financial / infrastructure stock that fits the long-horizon sovereign / financial-infrastructure theme. The PT PMA holds these shares in its own name through a stock account (rekening saham / participant account) at the IDX, funded directly from the Mandiri corporate account — no FX conversion to SGD needed, no cross-border transfer, no foreign brokerage. When the shares are sold, the capital gain is subject to a 0.1% final tax (PPh Final Pasar Modal) on the gross sale proceeds — this is the standard final withholding tax on share transactions on the Indonesia Stock Exchange, applied to the gross proceeds (not the net gain) and final (no additional income tax on the gain). No additional Indonesian tax on the conversion, because the money never leaves Indonesia — it goes from IDR cash → IDR-denominated shares → IDR cash, all within Indonesia and all within the PT PMA's name. The 0.1% final tax replaces the 22% Singapore capital gains tax in the earlier version of this strategy — and because the deployment is onshore and in IDR, there is no FX risk, no outbound dividend withholding, and no foreign jurisdiction tax to confirm. The 0.1% final tax, the exact share instrument (e.g. Mandiri shares vs another Indonesian-listed stock), and any dividend tax on share dividends (if the shares pay dividends, those dividends may be subject to a separate final tax — confirm with Indonesian counsel and the Mandiri relationship manager) must all be confirmed with Indonesian counsel and the Mandiri relationship manager before relying on them.

The after-tax IDR proceeds are used to purchase another Bali villa. A villa purchase is a capital purchase that counts towards the PT PMA's 10B IDR minimum paid-up capital / investment realisation requirement (KAP / government board), so each new villa both grows the portfolio and satisfies the Indonesian capital investment requirement. You can repeat this cycle: villa → rental → IDX Mandiri shares → sell → 0.1%-taxed IDR proceeds → next villa — each new villa both grows the portfolio and satisfies the Indonesian capital investment requirement. The PT PMA has a 3-year window to hit the 10B IDR capital investment requirement; extend to 4-5 years is possible with a documented investment plan and proof of progress, but the primary deadline is 3 years — plan the villa purchases so the 10B IDR is reached within the 3-year window (or file for the extension with the documented plan).
Core design principle: keep everything onshore in IDR. By deploying the after-tax rental IDR into Indonesian-listed domestic shares (e.g. Bank Mandiri / Mandiri shares, or another Indonesian-listed financial / infrastructure stock) through the IDX participant account — all in the PT PMA's name, all in IDR, all within Indonesia — the strategy avoids any cross-border FX conversion (no IDR→SGD step), any outbound dividend withholding (there is no Singapore shareholder and no dividend paid offshore — the money stays in Indonesia and is deployed as onshore equity, not paid out as a dividend), and any foreign capital gains tax (the only tax on the share sale is the 0.1% final tax on gross proceeds under PPh Final Pasar Modal — the standard final tax on Indonesia Stock Exchange share transactions — which replaces the 22% Singapore capital gains tax from the earlier version of this strategy). Step 1 — Register the PT PMA Indonesian foreign-owned LLC

A PT PMA (Penanaman Modal Asing) is Indonesia's vehicle for foreign direct investment. It is required for a foreigner to legally hold title/leasehold to Bali investment property, operate a rental business, and open a corporate bank account. The PT PMA is owned by the foreign shareholder (you / your structure) and managed by a local director or nominee manager (subject to Indonesian corporate law).

Do not skip the PT PMA. The villa cannot be held personally by a foreigner under Indonesian law (with limited exceptions). The PT PMA is the legally recognised vehicle. Trying to hold it informally exposes the structure to enforcement risk and can void the lease.

All villa rental income is deposited here; all operating expenses (management fees, utilities, maintenance, tax) are paid from here.

Risks & things to confirm before wiring

1. PT PMA structure. Minimum capital threshold, nominee director arrangement, KBLI code, local office requirement, and NPWP/NIB issuance all need confirmation with independent Indonesian counsel. The nominee director is a real risk — document control rights (shareholder authority, board resolutions, bank mandate) carefully. A poorly structured nominee arrangement can result in the director locking the entity (see the Phuket 13-foreigner case as a cautionary example of nominee structures gone wrong).
2. Bank account eligibility. Mandiri (and other Indonesian banks) have tightened KYC for foreign-owned PMA entities. Confirm that the PT PMA's structure qualifies for a corporate account — some banks require a minimum capital deposit, a local office address, or a certain shareholder structure. The bank opening can be the slowest step after the PT PMA is registered.
3. 10B IDR investment realisation deadline — the binding constraint. The PT PMA's minimum paid-up capital (10B IDR) must be invested in the business within 3 years of approval, per the KAP / government board requirement. If you cannot meet the 3-year deadline, you can apply to extend to 4–5 years — but you must document an investment plan and show proof of progress (e.g. villa leasehold signed, Mandiri corporate account opened, rental income starting). Buying a villa is a capital purchase that counts towards the 10B IDR PT PMA capital investment requirement. The practical implication: plan your villa purchases so the cumulative investment reaches 10B IDR within the 3-year window (or file for the extension with a documented plan and proof of progress). Do not leave the 3-year clock running without a villa purchase plan — the deadline is the binding constraint of the whole structure, not an afterthought. Confirm the exact deadline, extension process, and acceptable proof-of-progress evidence with Indonesian counsel.
4. IDX domestic-share dividend tax — separate from the 0.1% final tax on sale (onshore IDR / IDX domestic-shares version — current). The strategy deploys after-tax rental IDR as onshore equity into Indonesian-listed domestic shares (e.g. Mandiri shares) on the IDX — not as a cross-border dividend, so the Indonesia-Singapore DTA outbound dividend withholding (20% typical) does not apply. However, if the Indonesian-listed domestic shares pay dividends, those dividends may be subject to a separate final tax under PPh Final Pasal 4 ayat 2 — typically 10% for corporate recipients (confirm with Indonesian counsel and the Mandiri relationship manager). This is in addition to the 0.1% final tax (PPh Final Pasar Modal) on the share sale and the 10% rental tax already paid. Do not assume the dividend tax rate without confirmation — plan the exit structure in advance.
5. IDX domestic-share instrument and 0.1% final tax confirmation — PPh Final Pasar Modal on gross sale proceeds (onshore IDR / IDX domestic-shares version — current). The strategy names Bank Mandiri (Mandiri shares / Mandiri stock) as the example Indonesian-listed domestic share — a domestic Indonesian bank stock, IDR-denominated, listed on the Indonesia Stock Exchange (IDX / BEI). Confirm with the Mandiri relationship manager and Indonesian counsel: (a) the exact share instrument (Mandiri shares vs another Indonesian-listed financial / infrastructure stock — confirm the specific stock, its listing, its long-horizon growth profile relative to the Indonesian sovereign / financial-infrastructure theme); (b) the participant-stock-account onboarding (rekening saham / participant account at the IDX — confirm the sponsor / participant relationship through Mandiri, the account agreement, the Mandiri-IDX linkage, and any minimum-balance / trading requirements); (c) the 0.1% final tax (PPh Final Pasar Modal) on the sale of the shares — confirm the rate, that it is indeed final (no additional income tax on the gain), the taxpayer (the PT PMA), and the tax base (gross sale proceeds); and (d) any dividend tax on share dividends (if the shares pay dividends, those dividends may be subject to a separate final tax under PPh Final Pasal 4 ayat 2 — typically 10% for corporate recipients — confirm with Indonesian counsel and the Mandiri relationship manager; this is in addition to the 0.1% final tax on sale and the 10% rental tax already paid — do not assume the dividend is also 10% final without confirmation). No Singapore tax adviser is needed because there is no Singapore account, no Singapore holder, no Singapore tax — the sale is on the IDX in IDR in the PT PMA's name.
6. FX — not applicable to the onshore deployment (onshore IDR / IDX domestic-shares version — current). Because the strategy deploys the after-tax IDR directly into Indonesian-listed domestic shares (e.g. Mandiri shares / Mandiri stock, or another Indonesian-listed financial / infrastructure stock) on the IDX — with no IDR→SGD conversion and no cross-border transfer — there is no FX spread to build into the model and no Bank Indonesia / OJK outbound FX reporting for the deployment itself. The money stays in IDR and in Indonesia throughout: IDR cash → IDX shares → IDR cash. The IDR inflationary risk against SGD (flagged in the Bali strategy verdict) is a separate consideration if and when the PT PMA later wants to convert IDR to SGD for any reason (e.g. a cross-border expense), but for this onshore-share-deployment strategy, the FX risk does not apply to the deployment path. If at a later stage the PT PMA wants to convert IDR to SGD, the FX spread and outbound FX rules (Bank Indonesia / OJK) would then apply — confirm with the Mandiri relationship manager and Indonesian counsel. For the onshore deployment only: no FX, no outbound FX rules.
7. Indonesian FX outbound rules — not applicable to the onshore deployment (onshore IDR / IDX domestic-shares version — current). Bank Indonesia / OJK have reporting and, in some cases, restriction rules on capital repatriation by PMA entities. For the current onshore IDR / IDX domestic-shares version, there is no FX conversion (the after-tax IDR stays in IDR and is deployed directly into Indonesian-listed domestic shares on the IDX — e.g. Mandiri shares — no IDR-to-SGD step, no outbound transfer). The money stays in IDR and in Indonesia throughout: IDR cash → IDX shares → IDR cash → villa purchase. There is no cross-border transfer to confirm. If at a later stage the PT PMA wants to convert IDR to SGD for any reason (e.g. a cross-border expense), the outbound FX rules (Bank Indonesia / OJK) would then apply — confirm the current outbound FX rules for PMA entities with the bank's treasury desk and Indonesian counsel (the rules have shifted with the Omnibus Law and follow-on reforms). For the onshore deployment only: no outbound FX rules apply.

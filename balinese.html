Option 1 Bali — Income Strategy

PT PMA registration → Mandiri bank account → Singapore offshore IBKR · rental income 10% · deploy to SG without dividends · capital gains at 22%

Last updated:

Strategy overview the flow: villa → PT PMA → IDR bank → SGD IBKR

Rental income from the Bali villa lands in an Indonesian PT PMA bank account (Mandiri). A 10% final income tax is withheld on gross rental receipts. Rather than pay dividends to move money offshore (which triggers an additional tax layer), the after-tax IDR is converted to SGD and deployed into a Singapore-resident IBKR account to buy AI / robotics / sovereign-infrastructure shares. Capital gains on those shares are taxed at 22% in Singapore when realised — no additional Indonesian tax on the conversion or the investment, because the money is no longer Indonesian rental income by the time it reaches the SG brokerage.

The chain (5 steps)

  1. Register PT PMA — Indonesian foreign-owned LLC. Legal entity that can own leasehold villa, sign management agreement, receive IDR rental income, hold bank account, and file Indonesian tax.
  2. Register PT PMA bank account at Mandiri — corporate account in the PT PMA's name. All rental income flows here. 10% final tax withheld at source on gross rentals.
  3. Register Singapore offshore IBKR account — in the PT PMA's name (or the shareholder's, depending on IBKR's Singapore corporate onboarding). SGD-denominated. Receives converted IDR → SGD.
  4. Deploy into AI / robotics / sovereign-infrastructure shares — via IBKR. Capital gains taxed at 22% (Singapore) on realisation. No Indonesian tax on the gains — the rental income has already been taxed once in Indonesia at 10%.
  5. Sell SG shares on realisation (22% gain) → buy another Bali villa → counts toward 10B IDR PT PMA capital investment — when the SG IBKR portfolio has grown enough, sell shares (capital gains taxed at 22% in Singapore — corporate holder; individual may be exempt if gains are capital in nature — confirm with SG tax adviser). The after-tax SGD proceeds are converted back to IDR and used to purchase another Bali villa. A villa purchase is a capital purchase that counts towards the PT PMA's 10B IDR minimum paid-up capital / investment realisation requirement (KAP / government board), so each new villa both grows the portfolio and satisfies the Indonesian capital investment requirement. You can repeat this cycle: villa → rental → SG shares → sell → after-tax proceeds → next villa — each new villa both grows the portfolio and satisfies the Indonesian capital investment requirement. The PT PMA has a 3-year window to hit the 10B IDR capital investment requirement; extend to 4-5 years is possible with a documented investment plan and proof of progress, but the primary deadline is 3 years — plan the villa purchases so the 10B IDR is reached within the 3-year window (or file for the extension with the documented plan).
Core design principle: avoid the dividend trap. If the PT PMA pays a dividend to the Singaporean shareholder, Indonesia may apply an additional withholding tax on the outbound dividend (typically 20% under the Indonesia-Singapore DTA if conditions are met, otherwise the standard rate). The strategy instead converts rental income to SGD and deploys it as capital — the SG IBKR gains are a separate tax event (22%) in a separate jurisdiction. Confirm the Indonesia-Singapore DTA dividend withholding position with Indonesian counsel.

Step 1 — Register the PT PMA Indonesian foreign-owned LLC

A PT PMA (Penanaman Modal Asing) is Indonesia's vehicle for foreign direct investment. It is required for a foreigner to legally hold title/leasehold to Bali investment property, operate a rental business, and open a corporate bank account. The PT PMA is owned by the foreign shareholder (you / your structure) and managed by a local director or nominee manager (subject to Indonesian corporate law).

What a PT PMA gives you

CapabilityWhy you need it
Hold leasehold / HGB titleIndonesian law requires a PT PMA for foreign-owned property investment over a threshold. The villa leasehold is held in the PT PMA's name, not yours personally.
Sign management agreementThe operator (Balitecture, etc.) signs with the PT PMA — the entity that legally operates the rental. Your personal involvement is limited.
Receive IDR rental incomeRent goes to the PT PMA's bank account. The PT PMA is the taxable entity in Indonesia.
Open corporate bank accountMandiri (or BCA/Mandiri) corporate account in PT PMA name. Required to receive rental income and pay expenses.
File Indonesian corporate taxThe PT PMA files its own Indonesian corporate tax return. Rental income is taxed at the final 10% rate (see tax section).
Repatriate profits / capitalThe PT PMA can convert IDR → SGD and transfer to the Singapore IBKR account, subject to Bank Indonesia / OJK reporting rules on foreign capital repatriation.
Do not skip the PT PMA. The villa cannot be held personally by a foreigner under Indonesian law (with limited exceptions). The PT PMA is the legally recognised vehicle. Trying to hold it informally exposes the structure to enforcement risk and can void the lease.

Step 2 — Mandiri corporate bank account PT PMA · IDR · rental collection

Bank Mandiri is Indonesia's largest bank by assets and is commonly used by foreign-owned PT PMA entities. The PT PMA opens a corporate current account (rekening giro) in its name. All villa rental income is deposited here; all operating expenses (management fees, utilities, maintenance, tax) are paid from here.

What you get

FeatureDetail
Account currencyIDR (Rupiah). Rental income is IDR. Conversion to SGD happens at the FX stage before transfer to IBKR.
Account holderThe PT PMA (corporate account). Signatories: the local director / nominee + the foreign shareholder (depending on mandate).
Rental collectionThe property management/co-host (e.g. Airbnb payout, operator collection) deposits IDR into this account. Confirm the payout path with the operator — some use Airbnb's Indonesia payout; others collect locally and deposit manually.
Tax withholdingIndonesia's 10% final withholding tax on gross rental income is typically withheld at the source (by the payer or by the bank / tax office mechanism). Confirm with the tax adviser whether the 10% is withheld by the operator before deposit, or self-declared and paid by the PT PMA. The structure matters for cash flow planning.
Online banking / APIMandiri corporate online banking (M2U / Bizbanking) enables monitoring and transfers. IBKR transfer requires a USD/SGD FX conversion and a telegraphic transfer (TT) to the SG IBKR account — handled via the corporate online banking or a treasury desk.

The conversion rate you get from Mandiri's FX desk may differ from the interbank rate — build a 1–2% FX spread into the model. Confirm Mandiri's corporate FX minimums, TT fees, and any Indonesian FX outbound restrictions (Bank Indonesia / OJK rules on capital repatriation for PMA entities).

Step 3 — Singapore offshore IBKR account SGD · no Indonesian rental tax on gains

Interactive Brokers (IBKR) offers a Singapore-resident account in SGD. The PT PMA (or its Singapore-registered shareholder / holding entity) opens the account, funds it via IDR→SGD conversion from the Mandiri account, and deploys the capital into AI, robotics, sovereign-infrastructure, and related shares.

Why Singapore IBKR (not Indonesia)

ReasonDetail
SGD base currencyMatches the SGD income target. No unnecessary FX to AUD or USD if the deployment target is SGD-denominated assets.
Broad market accessIBKR SG gives access to SGX, US, HK, AU, and global equities — AI, robotics, semiconductor, sovereign-infrastructure exposure across markets from one custody account.
Tax clarity on gainsSingapore taxes capital gains at 22% (when realised, if the taxpayer is tax-resident in Singapore and the gains are not exempt under the capital gains regime). The rental income has already been taxed in Indonesia at 10% — the SG gains are a separate tax event. No double-taxation on the rental income itself.
No Indonesian tax on the gainsOnce the IDR is converted to SGD and leaves Indonesia, it is no longer Indonesian-sourced income. The IBKR gains are not Indonesian rental income — they are a capital gain in Singapore. Confirm with Indonesian counsel that the conversion + outbound transfer does not trigger an Indonesian exit tax or withholding (typically it does not, but the structure must be clean).
Avoids dividend routeThe alternative — paying a dividend from the PT PMA to the Singapore shareholder — may attract an Indonesia outbound dividend withholding tax (DTA rate 20% typical, subject to conditions). Deploying as capital (conversion + investment) avoids this layer.

If the holder is an individual, gains may be exempt (capital in nature) — but the 22% is used as the conservative planning rate. Confirm with SG tax adviser: individual vs corporate holder, and whether the gains are capital (exempt) or revenue (taxable at 17%/22%).

Tax comparison — dividend vs capital deployment 10% rental · 0% vs 22% on deployment

Two ways to move after-tax IDR rental income offshore to Singapore: (A) pay a dividend from the PT PMA to the Singapore shareholder, or (B) convert to SGD and deploy as capital into IBKR shares. The table shows the tax at each step.

1. Rental income received (gross IDR) 100% 100%
2. Indonesia 10% final withholding tax on rental −10% −10%
3. After-tax IDR (net rental) 90% 90%
4. Convert IDR → SGD (FX spread ~1–2%, not a tax) ~88–89% (after FX) ~88–89% (after FX)
5a. Outbound dividend withholding (Indonesia → SG) −20% typical (Indonesia-Singapore DTA, subject to conditions; verify) n/a — not a dividend
5b. Net SGD after dividend withholding ~70–71% of gross rental (after 10% + 20% + FX) —
6. Deploy SGD into IBKR shares (Option B) — ~88–89% deployed
Option C — Alternative: IDR stays in-country, invest in Local Market Mandiri Bank product paying ~11% dividend — ~90% stays in IDR (after 10% rental tax) → ~11% IDR yield on deployed balance — no FX, no SG tax, no 20% DTA — but IDR-denominated (inflation risk vs SGD), may be taxed separately at Indonesian corporate/final rate, lower growth ceiling than 15yr global share portfolio. Confirm instrument, yield terms, Indonesian tax treatment of the yield with Mandiri + counsel.
7. Capital gains tax on realisation (Singapore) 22% on gains (if corporate holder; individual may be exempt) 22% on gains (if corporate holder; individual may be exempt)
8. Total effective tax leakage on the rental income ~30%+ (10% rental + 20% dividend + FX drag) 10% rental only (FX drag 1–2%) — gains taxed separately at 22% when realised
The dividend route is the trap. A 10% rental tax is unavoidable in Indonesia. Adding a 20% outbound dividend withholding on the same money leaks ~30% of gross before it even reaches the investment account. Deployment as capital — convert, send to IBKR, buy shares — keeps the leakage at 10% + FX, with the 22% only applied to gains when realised. That is the core of this strategy.
Figures are illustrative model rates — 10% final rental withholding, 20% Indonesia-Singapore DTA dividend withholding (subject to conditions — verify with Indonesian counsel), 22% Singapore corporate tax on gains (individual may be exempt; verify with SG tax adviser), FX spread ~1–2% (Mandiri corporate FX). All figures must be confirmed with professionals before relying on them.

Risks & things to confirm before wiring

1. PT PMA structure. Minimum capital threshold, nominee director arrangement, KBLI code, local office requirement, and NPWP/NIB issuance all need confirmation with independent Indonesian counsel. The nominee director is a real risk — document control rights (shareholder authority, board resolutions, bank mandate) carefully. A poorly structured nominee arrangement can result in the director locking the entity (see the Phuket 13-foreigner case as a cautionary example of nominee structures gone wrong).
2. Bank account eligibility. Mandiri (and other Indonesian banks) have tightened KYC for foreign-owned PMA entities. Confirm that the PT PMA's structure qualifies for a corporate account — some banks require a minimum capital deposit, a local office address, or a certain shareholder structure. The bank opening can be the slowest step after the PT PMA is registered.
3. 10B IDR investment realisation deadline — the binding constraint. The PT PMA's minimum paid-up capital (10B IDR) must be invested in the business within 3 years of approval, per the KAP / government board requirement. If you cannot meet the 3-year deadline, you can apply to extend to 4–5 years — but you must document an investment plan and show proof of progress (e.g. villa leasehold signed, Mandiri corporate account opened, rental income starting). Buying a villa is a capital purchase that counts towards the 10B IDR PT PMA capital investment requirement. The practical implication: plan your villa purchases so the cumulative investment reaches 10B IDR within the 3-year window (or file for the extension with a documented plan and proof of progress). Do not leave the 3-year clock running without a villa purchase plan — the deadline is the binding constraint of the whole structure, not an afterthought. Confirm the exact deadline, extension process, and acceptable proof-of-progress evidence with Indonesian counsel.
4. Dividend withholding (Indonesia → Singapore). The 20% DTA rate is the typical outbound dividend withholding under the Indonesia-Singapore DTA, but it is subject to conditions (beneficial ownership, underlying tax, treaty claim procedure). The strategy assumes the dividend route is avoided entirely — deploy as capital instead. This must be confirmed: if the PT PMA later needs to pay a dividend (e.g. to distribute proceeds on exit), the withholding applies. Plan the exit structure in advance.
4. Singapore tax residency of the IBKR holder. The 22% capital gains rate assumes the account holder is a Singapore tax-resident company (or an individual whose gains are not exempt). If the holder is a non-resident or the gains are capital in nature (exempt for individuals), the effective rate differs. Confirm Singapore tax residency of the holder and the characterisation of the gains (capital vs revenue) with a Singapore tax adviser.
5. FX risk (IDR → SGD). The IDR is structurally inflationary against the SGD. The rental income is IDR; the deployment target is SGD. Every conversion loses value over time if the IDR weakens. Build a 1–2% FX spread per conversion into the model, and consider whether partial hedging (e.g. a USD/SGD intermediate) is warranted. This is the same currency risk flagged in the Bali strategy verdict — it applies to the deployment path as well.
6. Indonesian FX outbound rules. Bank Indonesia / OJK have reporting and, in some cases, restriction rules on capital repatriation by PMA entities. Converting IDR to SGD and transferring to Singapore may require a documented business purpose, source-of-funds declaration, and reporting. Confirm the current outbound FX rules for PMA entities with the bank's treasury desk and Indonesian counsel — the rules have shifted with the Omnibus Law and follow-on reforms.
7. IBKR onboarding source of funds. IBKR Singapore corporate onboarding requires a source-of-funds declaration. The Bali villa rental income is the source — document the villa, the PT PMA, the management agreement, and the Mandiri account as the source chain. Incomplete documentation can delay onboarding or trigger enhanced due diligence.
⚠ ACTION: Get independent Indonesian legal counsel (not the developer's notary) to confirm (a) PT PMA setup and minimum capital, (b) nominee director structure and control rights, (c) Mandiri corporate account eligibility and requirements, (d) Indonesia-Singapore DTA dividend withholding position and the capital-deployment alternative. Get a Singapore tax adviser to confirm (e) the IBKR holder's tax residency, (f) the 22% gains rate vs the capital-exemption position for an individual holder, and (g) the Singapore entity structure for the IBKR account.

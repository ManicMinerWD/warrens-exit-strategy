Key assumptions & things to confirm before relying on these figures

1. G05/108 purchase price. The $411,545 used as the cost base is the 2014 sale price, not necessarily Warren's 2017 purchase price. The actual 2017 settlement figure is needed to confirm the G05 capital gain (currently showing a small ~$8,455 gain). If the 2017 purchase was higher, the gain is smaller or could be a loss. ACTION: Confirm the 2017 settlement statement for G05.
2. Carried-forward rental losses. The ~$156,000 figure is an estimate built from the known rent trajectory and the current annual loss. The exact amount depends on the losses actually claimed in each year's tax return (some years may have had different rent, different expenses, or the losses may have been used against other income). ACTION: Get the tax adviser to confirm the exact carried-forward amount available to offset the Sussex St CGT.
3. Loss offset eligibility. The losses must be “rental property losses” (negative gearing / net capital cost) to offset a capital gain. If some losses are from a different category, they may not be eligible. The 302 capital loss ($22,045) is a genuine capital loss from selling below cost — that can offset any capital gain. ACTION: Confirm with the tax adviser that the carried-forward rental losses can offset the Sussex St CGT, and that the 302 capital loss is applied correctly.
4. 6-year main-residence exemption. Sussex St is assumed to have been Warren's main residence for 2007–2012 (6 years), then an investment property from 2012. The exemption fraction is 6/17 = 35.3% of the gain. This needs to be confirmed against the actual dates of main-residence use and the tax adviser's calculation. If the main-residence period was shorter or longer, the exempt portion changes. ACTION: Confirm the exact main-residence period and the exemption calculation.
5. 50% CGT discount. Applies because all three properties have been held for >12 months (G05 and 302 since 2017, Sussex since 2007). This is straightforward but should be confirmed if any property was used for income-producing purposes before being held as a main residence (the “periods of use” rules can affect the discount).
6. Marginal rate 47%. Assumes the gains fall in the top marginal bracket (45% + 2% Medicare levy). If Warren's other income in the year of sale is lower, the effective rate may be lower. The CGT is calculated on the taxable amount, not the total gain, so the rate applies to the post-discount, post-exemption figure.
7. Sussex St renovation cost. $40,000 is the estimated cost to uplift the property before sale (decking, bathroom + kitchen countertops, flooring, painting, gas-to-electric conversion, staging). If the actual renovation cost is higher, it increases the cost base (reducing the gain) but also reduces the net cash. If lower, the gain is higher. ACTION: Confirm the renovation budget and scope.
8. Sale prices are indicative. G05 and 302 are shown at the REA mid estimate ($420k each). The actual sale price may be higher or lower — off-market sales can achieve market or above if the buyer is motivated. Sussex St is shown at the agent midpoint ($865k); the actual sale could be anywhere in the $830k–$900k range (or beyond). The sensitivity table on sussex.html covers the $830k–$900k range for Sussex alone; for the combined exit, each $10k change in Sussex sale price changes the net cash by ~$10k (less ~$4.7k CGT if in the taxable range).
⚠ ACTION: Get the tax adviser to review this entire calculation BEFORE listing any property. The three items that most affect the final figure are: (1) the exact carried-forward rental loss amount, (2) confirmation that the losses can offset the Sussex St CGT, and (3) the G05 2017 purchase price. These three items determine whether the final net cash is ~$625k (full CGT) or ~$694k ($0 CGT) — a ~$70k difference.
